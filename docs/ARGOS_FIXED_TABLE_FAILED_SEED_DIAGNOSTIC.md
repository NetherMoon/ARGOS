## Tracking-direction source audit

`src/argos/vnext/mechanisms.py:151-199` constructs *both* symmetric normalized Pbar probes (`axis="P"`) and conditional-R probes (`axis="conditional_R"`) for each measured anchor. Pbar is reconstructed inside the existing legal coupled domain; R is recomputed from the conditional coordinate and `domain.r_max(Pbar)`. The targeted mechanism is therefore **not structurally R-only** and its name is accurate. Additional Pbar movement arises from V3, local, independent, and boundary probes.

`src/argos/vnext/controller.py:224-299` pools the targeted probes with local and boundary proposals, predicts and correction-scores them, explicitly reserves a targeted slot, then fills the remaining guided slots by the same feasibility-first score subject to distance deduplication. The score ranks corrected predicted constraint violations first; when no qualified bid exists, it next ranks the largest corrected constraint ratio. It does not reserve separate slots for Pbar and R.

The saved six-episode record has **43 evaluated tracking-direction probes, all conditional-R**: 23 for `W2-short-qos5_4.5_4_3.5` and 20 for `W2-short-qos5555`. This is a proposal-selection outcome, not evidence that Pbar probes were absent. Reconstructing the failed episode's batches 2-4 from the saved observations and immutable V3 bank reproduced every selected candidate ID and axis exactly. In those batches the mechanism generated 8 Pbar probes per batch and 7, 8, and 8 R probes respectively. The best Pbar probe ranked 6th, 11th, and 11th among targeted candidates; its corrected prediction still violated a constraint. The best R probe ranked 1st, 1st, and 2nd, with corrected predicted violation zero. Batch 5 again generated 8 Pbar and 8 R probes; its best Pbar probe ranked 11th and its best R probe 2nd. Scoring and the finite slot budget explain the observed R-only evaluated subset. This audit does not prove the predictions were accurate; the original near misses show substantial residuals.

## Necessary non-scientific fix and next protocol

The frozen bank's JSON manifest stores `weight_bounds` as a list, while the prior recovery identity used a Python tuple. That prevented a later process from reusing a valid saved bank. `src/argos/fixed_table/runner.py` now serializes the identity with a list; the bank ID and candidates are unchanged. The new validation also accepts explicit frozen plan paths while retaining the original 32-call/three-check episode protocol.

The prepared clean W2 plan uses three new arrival seeds for both workloads, the established fixed search-runtime seed, and three new runtime-check seeds. It has six independent searches, maximum 192 search calls and 18 checks, 210 total. Each arrival table gets its own selected bid. No validation simulation has been run.
