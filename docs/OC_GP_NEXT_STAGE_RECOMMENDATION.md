# GP next-stage decision after OC1.3

**Recommendation: GP OPTIONAL ABLATION ONLY; do not integrate a GP/residual model as the next required ARGOS-OC component.**

The decision is evidence-based, not a claim that a GP cannot help. A frozen eight-of-ten candidate was found in each of two clean cold searches with 56 calls to first eligibility. One appeared at 563.7 search seconds; the dedicated ten-minute run appeared at 623.0 seconds, 23 seconds beyond its soft target. The selected cold bid came directly from the V3 initial screen in both runs. The longer cold run made 72 additional calls without improving it. Warm structured search found nine-of-ten bids, and its objective-selected bid passed 28/30 independent fresh arrival/runtime pairs. The 8/10 cold bid itself has no held-out result.

These observations show that better simulator-call allocation, early rejection, and full worker use can reach the finite-panel target without a new learned component. The strict ten-minute target is not yet repeatably demonstrated, and bank-ready timing excludes the historical 350.65-second CPU V3 bank-generation cost. A GP might reduce calls or tighten the margin, but the current data do not establish the locally smooth simulator residuals a small GP would need. A 0.01 weight transfer changed the dominant QoS outcome sharply, and the cold winner's eighth-scenario margin was only 0.016667. It would be premature to add model complexity and claim an efficiency gain from these observations alone.

If the lab wants a later GP efficiency ablation, preregister a fixed cost and comparison protocol against this frozen OC1.3 method. Fit only on measured simulator residuals, initially for tracking p90 and the actually active QoS constraints (Bloom in v1; ResNet in the OC1.2 fresh assessment; GPT2 in one cold-panel failure). Do not feed raw integer seed values as numeric predictors. Use measured arrival descriptors such as per-type counts, interarrival summaries, burstiness, and late arrivals if cross-scenario modeling is justified. Require independent cold starts, equal FlexDC-call and wall-time accounting, and held-out arrival/runtime assessment of selected bids. Do not tune to the ten frozen search seeds or claim a population reliability probability from their pass count.

Before that ablation, the smaller research gap is to assess the cold-screen 8/10 bid on predeclared fresh scenarios and replicate timing on the intended hardware. Those are proposed future tests, not experiments performed here. The current defensible claim is that the revised OC search found common finite-panel feasible bids, including a 9/10 warm bid with 28/30 observed fresh-pair passes, while a separate clean 10-minute run narrowly missed its time target.
